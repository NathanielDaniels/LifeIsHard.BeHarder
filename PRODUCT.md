# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Subscribers to Patrick Wingert's email list: family, friends, sponsors, the Dare2Tri community, and fans who followed the Trans Bhutan Trail story. They open his emails on phones, between things, because they care about *him* — not triathlon generally. Sponsors read them to see their investment working. Locals read race-week emails to decide whether to show up.

## Product Purpose

patrickwingert.com and its email program tell the story of an adaptive triathlete — sobriety, amputation, Bhutan, elite racing — with live biometric data and cinematic presentation. Emails are the recurring touchpoint: race invites, race reports, season updates. Success = people showing up on course, sponsors renewing, and every email feeling like an event, not a newsletter.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, defiant, earned. "Life is Hard. Be Harder." Netflix-sports-doc opening credits; more Monocle than ESPN, but never sterile. Dark, moody, high-energy with breathing room. Short sentences that hit hard. The heartbeat/ECG motif ties everything to a living, measured body.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI-editorial**: tracked mono eyebrows over every section, hairline rules everywhere, numbered section markers as scaffolding, tasteful-but-soulless typography grids. (Two 2026-08 demo emails failed exactly this way — confirmed by Nathaniel.)
- **Cold and sparse**: stripping warmth/glow/richness until the email reads as a document instead of an event.
- **Flat hierarchy**: long scrolls where every section carries the same weight and nothing climaxes.
- **Template newsletter**: uniform rounded white cards on cream, identical section skeletons reused verbatim across sends.
- Anything that doesn't feel like patrickwingert.com's near-black cinematic identity.
- Copy written *in Patrick's voice by anyone but Patrick*. His draft copy ships verbatim; invented lines are the single most common complaint.

## Design Principles

1. **Every email is an event.** It should feel like a fight poster or broadcast graphic someone made for *this race*, not an issue of a recurring newsletter.
2. **Photography carries the emotion.** Patrick mid-race is the argument; type and color frame it. Grade photos cinematically rather than dropping them in raw.
3. **One climax per email.** Build an arc — loud open, useful middle, emotional peak, decisive close. Vary section treatments so the scroll has rhythm.
4. **Useful is premium.** The spectator card (wave, time, cap color, where to stand) is the payload; presenting logistics beautifully IS the brand flex.
5. **Chicago through motif, not costume.** For Chicago races: the flag's four red Municipal Device stars and its stripe structure enter as a graphic system, anchored by Patrick's black/orange/Bebas identity. Never invent civic symbolism — use the real thing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Live HTML text for all information; never bake critical facts into images (image-blocking clients, screen readers).
- Contrast ≥4.5:1 for body text on both dark and light sections.
- Meaningful alt text in Patrick's voice on every image.
- Singular "leg"/prosthetic-side language conventions per athlete context; respectful, non-inspirational-cliché framing of disability.
